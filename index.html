The physics behind the mission

The simulator uses the ideal two-body approximation, circular starting and ending orbits, and instantaneous coplanar burns.

01

Orbital radius

r = R + h

Altitude is measured from the surface, while orbital equations require distance from the body's center.

02

Circular velocity

v = √(μ / r)

Orbital speed depends on the gravitational parameter μ and orbital radius r.

03

Vis-viva equation

v = √[μ(2/r − 1/a)]

This gives spacecraft velocity at any point along the elliptical transfer orbit.

04

Transfer time

t = π√(a³ / μ)

A Hohmann transfer takes half of the elliptical transfer orbit's full period.

Project information

Built to make orbital mechanics visible

Orbit Transfer Lab is an independent aerospace engineering project created by Ved Patel. It connects physics, programming, and visual design to help students understand how spacecraft change circular orbits.

The model is educational and idealized. It does not include atmospheric drag, plane changes, finite burn duration, orbital perturbations, launch windows, or real mission constraints, and should not be used for operational mission planning.
